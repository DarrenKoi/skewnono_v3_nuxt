# UI/UX patterns for expert-facing engineering data-analysis tools (semiconductor metrology / yield / equipment), state of practice 2026 — external evidence

Evidence tags used on every finding: **[E]** empirical study (design and n given where known) · **[O]** expert opinion / practitioner guidance · **[V]** vendor documentation or product material (describes what a product does, not that it works) · **[S]** seen only through a search-result summary or secondary page, primary text not opened — verify before quoting.

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope is external evidence only; nothing here describes any specific company's internal systems.

## 1. Expert analytic workflows: sense-making loop, coordinated views, small multiples, drill-down, and dashboard failure modes

### Takeaway
The canonical models (Shneiderman's mantra, Pirolli & Card's foraging/sense-making loops) are frameworks and expert opinion, not measured effects; the hard evidence that exists says (a) dashboards built for monitoring serve far narrower needs than their users actually have, and (b) linked brushing only helps when the highlight is conspicuous and persistent — it is routinely overlooked or misunderstood. The safest reading for an expert tool: support overview → filter → detail and the reverse path (start from a known lot/tool and work outward), and make every cross-view link visibly obvious.

### Cited Findings
- **[O]** Shneiderman (1996, IEEE Symposium on Visual Languages) proposes "overview first, zoom and filter, then details-on-demand" and a taxonomy of seven tasks: overview, zoom, filter, details-on-demand, **relate, history, extract**. The abstract itself calls the mantra only "a starting point". The last three tasks (relate items, keep an action history, extract a sub-collection) are the ones dashboards most often omit. — [Shneiderman, "The Eyes Have It", UMD DRUM](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/handle/1903/466)
- **[E, cognitive task analysis]** Pirolli & Card (2005) split analyst work into a *foraging loop* (search and filter a large set down to relevant items) and a *sense-making loop* (move between relevant data and hypotheses), and state the process runs both bottom-up (information → schema → insight → product) and top-down (from a hypothesis back to data). — [Pirolli & Card, "The Sensemaking Process and Leverage Points for Analyst Technology" (course-hosted PDF)](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~mueller/teaching/cse591_visAnalytics/sensemaking.pdf) **[S]**
- **[O, survey]** Roberts (2007), "State of the Art: Coordinated & Multiple Views in Exploratory Visualization": the field had matured over ~15 years; "some may say that it is a solved problem, while others argue that we are only just scratching the surface". Rationale: several tightly coupled views of the same data let the user understand it from different perspectives. — [Roberts 2007, Kent Academic Repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/14569) **[S]**
- **[E, small n]** Evidence on brushing-and-linking is modest and mixed. In a stock-market exploratory-search study only 6 of 11 participants in one group could explain how brushing-and-linking worked, and participants said the highlight was too small and was overlooked. — [Visualizations in Exploratory Search: A User Study with Stock Market Information, arXiv 1209.2244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.2244) **[S]**
- **[E]** In a collaborative setting, transient brushing-and-linking gave less awareness than a persistent-selection display and "caused substantial interference". — [Awareness and distraction in loosely-coupled collaborative brushing and linking, UVic](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/5bc011be-b680-4f81-be49-9287b32e3097/full) **[S]**
- **[E, interview study]** Tory, Bartram, Fiore-Gartland & Crisan (IEEE CG&A 2021): dashboard users "have far more diverse needs than dashboards are typically designed for"; their engagement with data was "often intermittent, dependent on experts, and involved an awkward assembly of tools". — [Finding Their Data Voice: Practices and Challenges of Dashboard Users, Tableau Research](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/finding-their-data-voice-practices-and-challenges-dashboard-users) **[S]**
- **[E, corpus + literature review]** Sarikaya, Correll, Bartram, Tory & Fisher (IEEE InfoVis 2018 / TVCG 25(1), 2019) reviewed dashboards in practice, built a design space (design goals, levels of interaction, surrounding practices) and argue dashboards differ from exploratory visualization tools in design and context of use and need their own treatment. — [What Do We Talk About When We Talk About Dashboards? (PDF)](https://tableau.com/sites/default/files/2023-01/DashboardsConspiracy_final.pdf) **[S — PDF returned 403; only abstract-level content seen]**
- **[O]** NN/g (Kaplan, 2020) defines a complex application as one "supporting broad, unstructured goals or nonlinear workflows" for highly trained users, and gives 8 guidelines; the ones most relevant to analysis tools: (3) flexible, non-linear pathways — let users skip ahead, loop back; (4) help users track actions and thought processes (notes that survive interruption); (5) coordinate transitions to other tools (export data to Excel, images to PowerPoint, "copy visual as image"); (6) reduce clutter without reducing capability through staged disclosure; (7) show secondary detail without leaving the main screen (hover for exact values); (8) make important data salient by *removing* non-essential elements. The article's evidence base is thin: product examples plus links to NN/g's own earlier work, no named studies. — [8 Design Guidelines for Complex Applications, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/complex-application-design/)
- **[E, n=?, controlled]** Small multiples vs overlay — Javed, McDonnel & Elmqvist (IEEE TVCG 16(6), InfoVis 2010): shared-space charts (simple overlaid line graph, braided graph) were faster for *local* comparisons (find the maximum at one point in time); split-space charts (small multiples, horizon graphs) were faster for *dispersed* comparisons (discriminate values across different time positions). More concurrent series reduced correctness and increased time (N main effect F=858.92, p≤.001). Total chart size had no significant effect on time but shrinking it did reduce correctness. Simple line graphs and small multiples were the most robust across tasks; horizon graphs were slowest on the easiest task. Main study capped at 8 series; an informal 4-person follow-up to 16 series showed split-space scaling better. — [Graphical Perception of Multiple Time Series (PDF)](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/multilinevis.pdf)
- **[E]** Small multiples are not unlimited: an IEEE VIS 2024 study found accuracy on line-chart judgments declined roughly linearly as the number of frames grew. — [Examining Limits of Small Multiples: Frame Quantity Impacts Judgments with Line Graphs, IEEE VIS 2024](https://aa.ieeevis.org/year/2024/program/paper_v-tvcg-20243372620.html) **[S]**
- **[E, n=51 online]** Franke et al. (2022): as the number of series grows, overlaid techniques suffer more overlap and disorder while juxtaposed techniques hold up better; line charts and small multiples were often answered faster than horizon or braided graphs. — [SciTePress 2022 paper](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2022/107617/107617.pdf) **[S]**
- **[O, vendor docs]** Alert fatigue: Datadog's guidance is to review monitors continuously, lengthen evaluation windows, add recovery thresholds, group notifications, and mute during planned maintenance ("downtimes"). — [Best practices to prevent alert fatigue, Datadog](https://datadoghq.com/blog/best-practices-to-prevent-alert-fatigue/) **[S]**
- **[O]** Google SRE practice (Wilkinson, USENIX LISA 2018): alert on *symptoms* against a pre-declared acceptable level, not on causes; cause metrics belong on dashboards. — [SLO Burn — Reducing Alert Fatigue and Maintenance Cost in Systems of Any Size, USENIX LISA18](https://www.usenix.org/conference/lisa18/presentation/wilkinson) **[S]**
- **[O, vendor docs]** Dashboard sprawl: Grafana's guide says to prevent sprawl with template variables (one parameterised dashboard instead of one per node), build *hierarchical* dashboards that drill down to the next level, and that "most dashboards should be linked to by alerts. Browsing is directed with links." — [Grafana dashboard best practices (v13.1)](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/v13.1/visualizations/dashboards/build-dashboards/best-practices.md) **[S]**

### Inferences
- A tool-group → tool → lot → wafer → site → image hierarchy maps directly onto Grafana's "hierarchical drill-down, directed by links" advice and onto Shneiderman's mantra, but Pirolli & Card's top-down path means engineers will just as often *enter* at the bottom (a known lot or wafer). Every level should therefore be reachable directly and should link upward as well as downward.
- Because brushing is easily missed, cross-view selection in an expert tool should be persistent (a visible "selected: tool X / lot Y" chip that stays until cleared) rather than hover-only, and the highlight should dim non-selected marks rather than merely outline selected ones.
- Javed et al. gives a usable rule: overlay when the engineer compares a handful of series at the same x-position (≤ about 8, e.g. tools in one group at one date); switch to small multiples when comparing shapes across many tools or across different time ranges. The 2024 result argues for capping or paginating the grid rather than showing 40 panels at once.
- "Too many filters" as a failure mode is supported only indirectly here (NN/g staged disclosure; Tory et al.'s "more diverse needs than designed for"). Treat it as expert opinion.

### Gaps
- Stephen Few's "13 common pitfalls in dashboard design" and Munzner's *Visualization Analysis and Design* could not be opened; only book blurbs surfaced. Few's and Munzner's specific rules are therefore not cited here.
- The full Sarikaya et al. taxonomy (dashboard cluster names, the list of challenges such as lack of context or inflexibility) was not retrievable (403).
- No controlled study was found that measures the benefit of coordinated multiple views on expert task time; the support is survey and small-study level.

## 2. From "showing data" to "answering a question": exceptions, baselines, automatic comparison

### Takeaway
The proven interaction shape in adjacent industries is "select or detect the odd region → the tool automatically contrasts it with a baseline and ranks which dimensions differ". The pattern is widely shipped (Honeycomb BubbleUp, Datadog Watchdog Explains) and is the commercial yield tools' "commonality analysis" under another name, but quantified time-to-insight evidence in public sources is weak — vendor claims only.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Honeycomb BubbleUp: the user draws a box on a heatmap; the tool builds a comparison between the selection and everything else (the baseline) and renders one small chart per field, split into *Dimensions* (categorical) and *Measures* (numeric), selection in one colour and baseline in another; fields whose distributions differ most are the ones to look at next. — [Honeycomb docs: Identify outliers (BubbleUp)](https://docs.honeycomb.io/investigate/analyze/identify-outliers/)
- **[V]** Datadog Watchdog Explains: anomaly detection runs on dashboard time-series, comparing against history with up to three weeks of look-back for seasonality (a Monday 09:00 spike is compared with previous Mondays at 09:00 and not flagged if it recurs); the anomalous region is boxed on the chart, and tag groups that "significantly contributed to the shape or scale" are listed; clicking a tag shows the graph with that dimension isolated or removed. — [Datadog docs: Watchdog Explains](https://docs.datadoghq.com/dashboards/graph_insights/watchdog_explains)
- **[V]** PDF Solutions' wafer-map material lists automated excursion alerts, trend charts by layer, and aggregation across products / wafers / tools / modules as the non-spatial complement to maps, plus Bin Pareto to rank top failing bins. — [Understanding Semiconductor Data Visualization with Wafer Maps – An Introduction, PDF Solutions, 2025-07-02](https://www.pdf.com/understanding-semiconductor-data-visualization-with-wafer-maps-an-introduction)
- **[V]** An Intel presentation at PDF Solutions' 2025 user conference lists Commonality Analysis as a use case, with a slide titled "Test Bin to Tool Commonality" pairing wafer-map visualisation with wafer → tool commonality. — [Intel: Leveraging PDF for Advanced Packaging and Test Manufacturing Data Analytics (PDF)](https://www.pdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/19-Intel-Leveraging-PDF-for-Intel-Advanced-Packaging-and-Test-Manufacturing-Data-Analytics-_-Syed-Baquar.pdf) **[S]**
- **[V]** Annotated charts: Grafana renders annotations as vertical lines and icons across all graph panels, so one event (deploy, maintenance) is visible on every chart at once. — [Grafana docs: Annotate visualizations (v13.1)](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/v13.1/dashboards/build-dashboards/annotate-visualizations/) **[S]**
- **[O]** NIST e-Handbook: adding the four WECO run rules to a 3-sigma Shewhart chart raises the false-alarm rate from about one in 371 points to about one in 91.75 points (citing Champ & Woodall 1987); "some add the WECO rules but give their signals less weight during troubleshooting". — [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook 6.3.2, What are Variables Control Charts?](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc32.htm)
- **[O]** Datadog recommends anomaly / change / forecast monitors instead of static thresholds for metrics with time-based variation, and "alert liberally; page judiciously". — [Datadog: Best practices to prevent alert fatigue](https://datadoghq.com/blog/best-practices-to-prevent-alert-fatigue/) **[S]**

### Inferences
- An exception-first landing view for a metrology fleet is the direct analogue of these products: rank tools/recipes by deviation from a baseline (prior period, group median, or designated golden tool), and for each exception pre-compute "what is different about the affected measurements" across the available dimensions (tool, chamber, recipe, product, step, time since PM). This is BubbleUp's algorithm applied to fab context.
- The NIST false-alarm arithmetic is a concrete warning for exception lists: every extra rule multiplies noise. A ranked list with severity tiers (3-sigma violation above run-rule hits) follows NIST's "less weight" advice and Datadog's liberal-alert / selective-page split.
- PM / recipe-change / calibration events drawn as shared vertical markers on every trend chart is the lowest-cost "what changed" aid, and is directly supported by ECharts (`markLine`) and by the Grafana precedent.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed measurement of time-to-insight reduction from exception-first or auto-explanation views was found. Honeycomb and Datadog claims are vendor material.
- No public source describing how KLA Klarity / 5D Analyzer, Applied E3 or Synopsys/Siemens yield tools present ranked exceptions in the UI was retrievable; these products document features behind customer logins.

## 3. Semiconductor-specific visualisation conventions and what commercial fab analytics tools do

### Takeaway
Public documentation confirms a stable vocabulary — bin / parametric / defect / overlay maps, stacked maps, zonal (radial ring, quadrant, column, custom) summaries, Pareto, trend-by-tool, commonality, point-and-click from map to image gallery — and two rules that matter for correctness: normalise every map to one notch/axis convention before stacking, and never treat within-wafer sites as independent subgroups in SPC. Detailed UI behaviour of KLA, Applied and Synopsys/Siemens products is not publicly documented.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Wafer-map types per PDF Solutions (2025): **bin maps** (categorical; white = pass, colours = failure mechanisms), **parametric maps** (continuous values, colour gradient, blue→yellow in their example), **defect maps** (location, by layer, shape/colour by defect type), **bin-defect overlay maps** with overlay statistics (pass-clean / pass-defect / fail-clean / fail-defect, giving kill ratio and capture rate). — [PDF Solutions wafer map introduction](https://www.pdf.com/understanding-semiconductor-data-visualization-with-wafer-maps-an-introduction)
- **[V]** Zonal analysis divides the wafer into circular, quadrant or column zones and computes a statistic per zone; a zonal editor lets engineers pick a predefined pattern, adjust boundaries or define custom zones. A "defect die map" stacks all dies to show whether defects recur at the same in-die position. Microscope images for a defect can be displayed directly from the map. — [PDF Solutions wafer map introduction](https://www.pdf.com/understanding-semiconductor-data-visualization-with-wafer-maps-an-introduction)
- **[V]** Exensio Defect Management lists stacked defect maps, zonal maps, charts and plots, with "semiconductor-specific visualizations" and preconfigured templates; analyses can be saved as templates for repeat use; a spatial signature library is offered. — [PDF Solutions Defect Management brochure (PDF)](https://www.pdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PDF_Defect_Management_re_4_Tibco_no_nda.pdf) **[S]**
- **[V]** yieldWerx documents a stacked wafer map over all wafers for bins/soft bins and parametric zonal analysis. — [yieldWerx Yield Management Reporting and Analysis System](https://yieldwerx.com/?p=1199) **[S]**
- **[V, dated 2011]** KLA Klarity LED: defect image review with **point-and-click access from the wafer map** and an **image gallery** to validate classification; spatial signature analysis with stacked wafer signatures; repeater detection within and across wafers; automated report generation. — [KLA-Tencor launches Klarity LED, KLA investor relations](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/250/kla-tencor-launches-klarity-led-defect-analysis-system) **[S]**
- **[V, patent]** KLA holds a patent on searching through and analysing defect images and wafer maps (search-by-similarity over image/map sets). — [US7283659B1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7283659) **[S — title only]**
- **[O]** Orientation: wafers are tested in different orientations on different equipment and programs, so a per-product "topology specification" (notch angle, X/Y direction) is used to transform all data into one coordinate system before plotting; the convention described places the notch at the bottom with X increasing right and Y increasing up. — [LTIMindtree, Automated Test Data Collection for Yield Improvement using Wafer Mapping Analysis](https://www.ltimindtree.com/?p=15474) **[S]**
- **[O]** SPC rules (NIST): WECO — one point beyond ±3σ; 2 of 3 beyond ±2σ same side; 4 of 5 beyond ±1σ same side; 8 consecutive on one side. Trend rules — 6 in a row rising/falling; 14 alternating. Control limits answer "is the process consistent?", specification limits answer "will the product perform?" — they are different lines and should be drawn differently. — [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook 6.3.2](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc32.htm)
- **[V/O]** JMP's Control Chart Builder applies Western Electric / Nelson tests 1–8 and Westgard rules and **circles the points that trigger a test**; tests are toggled under Warnings > Tests. Users nest wafer within lot on the X axis. — [JMP Community: Where to find Nelson Rules in control chart in JMP 17](https://community.jmp.com/t5/Discussions/Where-to-find-Nelson-Rules-in-control-chart-in-JMP-17/m-p/634565/highlight/true) **[S]**
- **[O, practitioner forum]** Pitfalls reported by JMP users in semiconductor settings: sites within a wafer are highly correlated and should not set chart limits as if independent subgroups; a wafer can pass the control chart while its spatial pattern is biased, which a radial transform (value vs radius from centre) reveals; applying phases can stop run rules from firing across the phase boundary. — [JMP Community: Control Charts with Nelson rule n=3](https://community.jmp.com/t5/Discussions/Control-Charts-with-Nelson-rule-n-3/m-p/566213); [JMP Discovery abstract: More Unsupervised Engineering — Hacking the Short Run Control…](https://community.jmp.com/t5/Abstracts/More-Unsupervised-Engineering-Hacking-the-Short-Run-Control/ev-p/776134) **[S — attribution of each point to a specific thread not verified]**
- **[O]** Tool matching: Bland-Altman plots the difference between two methods against their mean; summary numbers are bias and limits of agreement (bias ± 1.96·SD). Correlation is not agreement — a worked example shows near-perfect r with a systematic bias. Check that spread is constant across the range (else log-transform) and show confidence intervals on the limits (SE ≈ SD·√(3/n)). — [Analyse-it: Bland-Altman limits of agreement](https://analyse-it.com/learn/bland-altman-limits-of-agreement); [Bland, lecture notes on limits of agreement, Univ. of York](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/msc/clinimet/week3/limagree.pdf) **[S]**
- **[V]** JMP ships the same chart as the "Tukey Mean-Difference" plot under Matched Pairs. For tool-to-tool matching a JMP community answer recommends variance components from a variability study, or a model with tool as a factor. — [JMP Community: How to create a Bland-Altman plot in JMP](https://community.jmp.com/t5/Discussions/How-to-create-a-bland-altman-plot-in-JMP/m-p/3594); [JMP Community: Tool to tool matching](https://community.jmp.com/t5/Discussions/Tool-to-tool-matching/m-p/16522) **[S]**
- **[E/O, SPIE]** CD-SEM fleet matching: a 2024 SPIE paper states a target that whole-fleet matching error stay below 10% of the process control window; a 2008 SPIE paper argues matching established on dedicated test structures may not hold on production layers. — [Data-driven CD-SEM fleet matching in sub-Å era, SPIE 12955 (2024)](https://journals.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12955/1295519/Data-driven-CDSEM-fleet-matching-in-sub-%c3%85-era/10.1117/12.3010756.full); [Physical matching of CD-SEM: noise analysis and verification, SPIE 6922 (2008)](https://ebooks.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6922/69221R/Physical-matching-of-CD-SEM--noise-analysis-and-verification/10.1117/12.776865.full) **[S — abstracts only]**
- **[O, patent text]** TMU (total measurement uncertainty) by itself "says nothing about how closely a tool under test agrees with a reference tool"; because letting any tool measure any step is cheaper, matching demands rise. — [US 7340374, Determining fleet matching problem and root cause issue for measurement system](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7340374); [US 7532999](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7532999) **[S]**

### Inferences
- Minimum wafer-map feature set that engineers will expect because every commercial tool has it: fixed, labelled notch orientation; categorical palette for bins vs sequential/diverging for parametric values; stack/composite over a selection of wafers; ring and quadrant zonal summary beside the map; click a site to open its image(s); a radial plot (value vs radius) as the companion view.
- On control charts: draw spec limits and control limits with distinct line styles, mark rule-violating points directly on the chart (JMP's circle convention), and let users choose which run rules are active because of the 371 → 92 false-alarm cost.
- A tool-matching page that shows only correlation or a mean-offset bar is weaker than the measurement-agreement literature recommends; a mean-difference (Bland-Altman) plot per tool pair plus a tool × tool offset heatmap for the group covers both the pairwise and the fleet question. The heatmap form is an inference — no source found describing a "matching matrix" UI.
- Matching verified on reference structures should be shown separately from matching observed on production layers (SPIE 6922).

### Gaps
- No public UI documentation was found for KLA Klarity Defect / 5D Analyzer, Applied SmartFactory / E3, Synopsys (Odyssey/YieldExplorer) or Siemens yield tools; what engineers "rely on" in those products could not be evidenced beyond feature lists. Spotfire-in-fab practice was likewise not sourced.
- No SEMI standard text (e.g. on notch/flat orientation or map coordinate conventions) was retrieved; the notch-down convention above rests on one practitioner article.
- Box-plots-by-tool/chamber for commonality and Pareto charts for failures are named in vendor feature lists but no source describing UI best practice for them was found.
- No source found on colour-scale conventions specific to parametric wafer maps (e.g. spec-centred diverging scales).

## 4. Image-heavy workflows: browsing and comparing large sets of scientific / inspection images

### Takeaway
Radiology offers the best-evidenced transferable patterns: (1) a *hanging protocol* — a rule that automatically lays out the right images in the right viewports for a given study type — is valued more than any single viewer feature and cut interpretation time and clicks substantially in a controlled study; (2) viewport *synchronisation groups* (pan/zoom, contrast, position) are a standard, toggleable primitive; (3) for spotting differences between two images, flicker/blink in one screen position beat side-by-side in the one study found.

### Cited Findings
- **[E, survey of radiologists]** Automatic image organisation through hanging protocols was highly valued with an estimated 10–20% reduction in interpretation time; respondents estimated 10–15 protocols would cover about 85–95% of regular examinations. — [Journal of Digital Imaging, doi 10.1007/s10278-004-1003-9 (2004)](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10278-004-1003-9) **[S]**
- **[E, lab study n=20, novice subjects]** A staged, scenario-based extension of hanging protocols (HP++) gave a 15% reduction in average interpretation time, one third fewer interpretation errors, two thirds fewer mouse clicks, and over 65% less eye gaze on workstation controls versus a conventional interface; preliminary validation with physicians pointed the same way. — [Simon Fraser University PhD thesis, Summit repository](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/8691) **[S]**
- **[E, small survey n=28]** Among those who use hanging protocols, 74% said they are of poor quality — i.e. the pattern is valued but generic implementations fail when rules do not match real studies. — [Visage / SIIM survey results, AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/enterprise-imaging/pacs-vna/article/15606796/visage-siim-release-survey-results) **[S]**
- **[V, open source]** OHIF Viewer: hanging protocols are scored against the available image sets and the best match is applied; a protocol sets the layout (e.g. 2×2), which image set goes in which viewport (by metadata), initial display settings, and synchronisation rules. — [OHIF docs: Hanging Protocol module](https://v3-docs.ohif.org/platform/extensions/modules/hpModule) **[S]**
- **[V, open source]** OHIF synchronisation groups keep viewports in step on camera position, window level (contrast), zoom/pan and slice position; groups are defined in the protocol or toggled from a toolbar button; a viewport can be "source-only" (drives others without receiving). — [OHIF docs: SyncGroupService](https://docs.ohif.org/platform/services/data/SyncGroupService) **[S]**
- **[E, n=12, aerial imagery]** Alternating (flicker) presentation gave better change-detection performance than parallel side-by-side — higher hit rates, shorter completion time, lower perceived workload — for the large majority of data sets; adding an automated change mask raised hit rates further (up to 95% for image pairs). — [Fraunhofer Publica record (HIMI 2018)](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/550fbe2f-a76e-471d-9cc1-5a67e50454f1) **[S]**
- **[V]** Defect-review precedent: KLA Klarity LED couples the wafer map to an image gallery by point-and-click and uses the gallery to validate classification. — [KLA investor relations, 2011](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/250/kla-tencor-launches-klarity-led-defect-analysis-system) **[S]**
- **[V]** PDF Solutions' tools display microscope images for defects directly from the wafer map. — [PDF Solutions wafer map introduction](https://www.pdf.com/understanding-semiconductor-data-visualization-with-wafer-maps-an-introduction)
- **[O]** NN/g on detail-while-browsing: avoid modals for deep inspection because they hide adjacent records users routinely refer to; prefer a non-modal side panel or separate window. — [Data Tables: Four Major User Tasks, NN/g (Laubheimer, 2022)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/)

### Inferences
- A gallery of ~130 images per measurement is a "hanging protocol" problem: the default arrangement should come from metadata (e.g. site order, image type, slot) rather than file order, and a small number of named layouts will likely cover most uses (radiologists: 10–15 protocols for 85–95%).
- Compare mode should offer both linked side-by-side (synchronised pan/zoom, toggleable — the OHIF sync-group model) and a blink toggle on a single key that swaps two registered images in place; the flicker study suggests the latter is better for "what changed between tool A and tool B / before and after PM". A difference image is the analogue of the "change mask" that raised hit rates.
- The NN/g modal warning argues for a lightbox that keeps a filmstrip or the site map visible, with arrow-key navigation, rather than a full-screen modal that hides context.
- The 74% "poor quality" figure is a caution: automatic layouts must be overridable and the override should be remembered.

### Gaps
- No empirical source found on virtualised galleries, contact-sheet density, or keyboard navigation specifically; these remain engineering common practice without cited evidence here.
- No usability study isolating synchronised pan/zoom was found (the search surfaced only a patent).
- No source found from microscopy software (ImageJ/Fiji, OMERO, napari) or from SEM defect-review station UIs; measurement-cursor overlay practice is unsourced.
- The flicker result is from aerial imagery with 12 participants; transfer to SEM/AFM images is plausible but untested.

## 5. Time-series and telemetry

### Takeaway
The semiconductor FDC convention is to cut each trace into step-aligned windows and compare window statistics, and the public literature names manual window definition as the method's largest cost. On the display side, LTTB downsampling, shared cursors and cross-chart zoom/brush are all available off the shelf in ECharts; choice of overlay vs small multiples should follow the Javed et al. result in section 1.

### Cited Findings
- **[V, patent — PDF Solutions]** FDC "starts with breaking a complex trace into logical windows and then computing statistics on the trace data in the windows"; the statistics are tracked with SPC and used as inputs to predictive models and root-cause analysis. Figures show trace data over multiple process steps and the same data **aligned to the beginning of a step**. "Window definition is largely manual and is one of the key limitations and largest costs in the use of FDC techniques." — [US11687439B2, Automatic window generation for process trace](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11687439) **[S]**
- **[V]** camLine LineWorks FDC is a commercial product for real-time trace monitoring and FDC (no public UI detail). — [LineWorks FDC, camLine](https://www.elisaindustriq.com/camline/products/lineworks-fdc) **[S]**
- **[O/E, MSc thesis]** LTTB (Largest-Triangle-Three-Buckets), Steinarsson 2013, University of Iceland: buckets the data and keeps the most visually significant point per bucket to preserve the line's shape with far fewer points. — [Downsampling Time Series for Visual Representation, Skemman handle 1946/15343](https://hdl.handle.net/1946/15343) **[S]**
- **[O]** LTTB caveats: it is "not a technique for statistical aggregation" (do not compute statistics on downsampled data); the original implementation does not support gaps (nulls) and needs strictly increasing x. — [lttb, PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/lttb/); [highcharts-downsample README](https://github.com/vim-zz/highcharts-downsample) **[S]**
- **[V]** ECharts: line series support `sampling: 'lttb'` (off by default); the ECharts test suite shows LTTB respecting `connectNulls`. — [ECharts test: line-sampling.html](https://apache.googlesource.com/echarts/+/refs/heads/next/test/line-sampling.html) **[S]**
- **[V, third-party mirror of ECharts docs]** Performance guidance: `sampling: 'lttb'` for line charts above roughly 10,000 points, progressive rendering above roughly 3,000 elements, `large: true` above roughly 100,000 points; a `dataZoom` start/end window with `filterMode: 'weakFilter'` to show a slice initially. — [ECharts performance guide (Mintlify mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/apache/echarts/guides/performance) **[S — thresholds not verified against echarts.apache.org]**
- **[V, third-party mirror]** `echarts.connect` (array of instances or a shared `group` id) propagates tooltip, dataZoom, legend and brush actions across charts, so zooming or brushing one chart updates the others. — [ECharts connect API (Mintlify mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/apache/echarts/api/connect) **[S]**
- **[V]** Grafana precedent for synchronised cursors: a dashboard-level setting cycles default / shared crosshair / shared tooltip (introduced in v4.1); annotations appear as vertical lines on all panels. — [Grafana: What's new in v4.1](https://grafana.com/docs/v4.2/guides/whats-new-in-v4-1/); [Grafana: Annotate visualizations](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/v13.1/dashboards/build-dashboards/annotate-visualizations/) **[S]**
- **[E]** Overlay is faster for same-position comparisons, small multiples for dispersed ones, and performance degrades with more concurrent series. — [Javed, McDonnel & Elmqvist 2010 (PDF)](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/multilinevis.pdf)
- **[V]** Relating telemetry to an outcome: Honeycomb's selection-vs-baseline contrast and Datadog's tag-contribution analysis are the shipped patterns (see section 2). — [Honeycomb BubbleUp](https://docs.honeycomb.io/investigate/analyze/identify-outliers/); [Datadog Watchdog Explains](https://docs.datadoghq.com/dashboards/graph_insights/watchdog_explains)

### Inferences
- Step alignment should be a first-class x-axis mode ("time since step start") next to wall-clock time; the FDC patent's own figures use it and it is what makes run-to-run overlay meaningful.
- A golden-trace envelope (band from a reference set of good runs, current run drawn on top) is the natural visual form of "window statistics vs SPC limits"; no source was found documenting how commercial tools draw it, so treat the specific form as an inference.
- Downsample for drawing only; any statistic, limit check or export must use the raw points (LTTB caveat). Traces with gaps need the null-handling path verified.
- Relating telemetry to outcome: let the user brush a window on a trace and get a ranked list of sensors/steps whose window statistics differ between good and bad outcome groups — the BubbleUp shape applied to FDC windows.

### Gaps
- No public source found describing golden-trace/envelope UI conventions, nor Applied E3 / other FDC trace viewers' interaction design.
- Official ECharts option pages (echarts.apache.org) could not be fetched (JS-rendered / 404 on the handbook path tried); option names above come from the test suite and a third-party mirror.
- Current Grafana docs for the shared-crosshair setting were not retrieved; the citation is an old release note.

## 6. Workspace / IDE-shell patterns, saved views, deep links, collaboration and export

### Takeaway
Evidence here is almost entirely expert opinion. The best-supported items are NN/g's: expert tools need non-linear pathways, a way to record what the analyst did and thought, and deliberate hand-offs to Excel/PowerPoint; and users of analytics products explicitly ask for filter state in the URL so a view can be shared or bookmarked. Keyboard shortcuts are fast but under-used unless the UI teaches them.

### Cited Findings
- **[O]** NN/g guideline 4: help users track actions and thought processes — let them record notes so they can resume after interruption (example: TreeAge comments during modelling). Guideline 5: coordinate transitions among tools — export data to Excel, images to PowerPoint, Power BI's "copy visual as image". Guideline 3: avoid rigid linear workflows. — [8 Design Guidelines for Complex Applications, NN/g (2020)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/complex-application-design/)
- **[O]** NN/g on tables as work surfaces: support four tasks — find records matching criteria, compare data, view/edit a single row, act on records. Freeze header row and identifier column; make column hide/reorder easy and show an indicator when columns are hidden; show a clear cue when filters are active; prefer a non-modal panel over a modal or accordion for row detail because users routinely reference other rows; batch actions via row checkboxes. — [Data Tables: Four Major User Tasks, NN/g (2022)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/)
- **[E, user demand]** Adobe Analytics users request that dropdown and date-range changes be reflected in the URL because otherwise a view cannot be shared or bookmarked. — [Adobe Experience League: Analysis Workspace — deep linking to shared dashboards with preselection](https://experienceleaguecommunities.adobe.com/adobe-analytics-3/analysis-workspace-deep-linking-to-shared-dashboards-with-preselection-2941) **[S]**
- **[O]** Deep-linking pattern guidance: give every meaningful state its own URL; put navigation-critical state (position, filters, selection) in it and keep personal preferences out so a shared link does not change the recipient's settings. — [Deep linking pattern, Pattern Playground](https://pattern-playground.onrender.com/patterns/deep-linking) **[S — low-authority source]**
- **[E]** Hotkeys "allow fast interaction, but they are known to be infrequently used"; the ExposeHK technique overlays hotkeys on their commands when a modifier is held, to promote learning. — [Promoting Hotkey Use through Rehearsal with ExposeHK, HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01894253) **[S]**
- **[V]** Saved analyses as reusable templates are a stock feature in fab analytics (Exensio: "save any analysis in templates for automation or repeated analysis"). — [PDF Solutions Defect Management brochure](https://www.pdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PDF_Defect_Management_re_4_Tibco_no_nda.pdf) **[S]**
- **[V]** Automated report generation from the review workflow is a stated feature of KLA's defect-analysis software. — [KLA Klarity LED announcement](https://ir.kla.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/250/kla-tencor-launches-klarity-led-defect-analysis-system) **[S]**
- **[E, interview study]** Dashboard users' work "involved an awkward assembly of tools" — moving results between tools is where friction concentrates. — [Tory et al. 2021](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/finding-their-data-voice-practices-and-challenges-dashboard-users) **[S]**

### Inferences
- "Snapshot of a finding" = URL that restores the exact view + a short note + image copy for a slide. All three pieces have independent support (deep-link demand, NN/g guideline 4, NN/g guideline 5); combining them is an inference.
- Shneiderman's *history* and *extract* tasks (section 1) are the theoretical basis for session restore, recent items, and "export this selection as CSV".
- For a command palette, the ExposeHK finding implies shortcuts should be displayed next to commands in menus and tooltips; a hidden shortcut layer will go unused.
- When an IDE shell helps vs hurts: no evidence found. Reasoning from NN/g's definition, a tree + tabs + panels shell fits open-ended, non-linear work where several artefacts are open at once, and adds overhead for single-question monitoring pages.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found on IDE-style shells (file tree, tabs, panels) for data analysis, on notebooks vs fixed dashboards for engineers, on command-palette efficiency, or on the value of annotation/collaboration features to engineering users.
- No evidence found on PPT/Excel export usage rates in fab engineering workflows beyond NN/g's general recommendation and vendor feature lists.

## 7. Conversational / LLM assistance inside analytics tools (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Verification, not generation, is the bottleneck: studies show users hesitate to rely on LLM analysis they cannot check, structured step-wise output measurably improves control and verifiability, and benchmarks on realistic enterprise schemas and on chart reading show accuracy far below headline numbers. Patterns that keep the result inspectable (NL → an ordinary filtered view whose filters are visible and editable) are the defensible ones; free-text "explain this chart" from an image is the riskiest.

### Cited Findings
- **[E, formative n=15 + within-subjects n=18]** Kazemitabaar et al. (UIST 2024): a formative study found serious challenges verifying AI-generated results and steering the AI. Two designs that decompose the analysis — *Stepwise* (editable assumptions and code per sub-goal) and *Phasewise* (editable input/output assumptions, plan, then code) — gave significantly greater perceived control and easier intervention, correction and verification than a conversational baseline. — [Improving Steering and Verification in AI-Assisted Data Analysis with Interactive Task Decomposition, arXiv 2407.02651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02651)
- **[E, n=22 cited within the same paper line]** Users verifying an AI answer to a natural-language data query show procedure-oriented and data-oriented checking behaviours, often tightly coupled; offloading a whole analysis creates a much larger verification demand than conventional tool-assisted analysis. — [arXiv 2407.02651 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02651) **[S]**
- **[E]** In a study of human-LLM collaboration for domain-specific data analysis, trust was a central concern because participants struggled to verify the tool's results and hesitated to rely on it without independent verification. — [Investigating Interaction Modes and User Agency in Human-LLM Collaboration for Domain-Specific Data Analysis, arXiv 2405.05548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.05548) **[S]**
- **[O]** Bertini: natural-language requests are inherently ambiguous compared with explicit computational procedures, and showing generated code is a "very blunt" verification aid. — [Verification Is the Bottleneck in AI-driven Data Analysis, FILWD](https://filwd.substack.com/p/verification-is-the-bottleneck-in) **[S]**
- **[E, benchmark]** Spider 2.0 (632 real enterprise text-to-SQL workflow problems, databases often over 1,000 columns): an agent framework with o1-preview solved about 17–21% of tasks, versus 91.2% on Spider 1.0 and 73.0% on BIRD. — [Literature review of Spider 2.0, Moonlight](https://themoonlight.io/review/spider-20-evaluating-language-models-on-real-world-enterprise-text-to-sql-workflows) **[S — secondary summary; two figures (17.0% and 21.3%) reported for different subsets/versions]**
- **[E, benchmark, Sept 2025]** ChartHal (1,062 human-validated samples) reports GPT-5 at 34.46% and o4-mini at 22.79% accuracy on its chart-understanding hallucination test; questions about information absent from or contradicting the chart are especially likely to trigger hallucination. — [ChartHal, arXiv 2509.17481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17481) **[S]**
- **[O, vendor/consultancy]** Conversational-BI adoption lessons: start with analysts/power users; a semantic layer is a real modelling investment (weeks per domain); output is only as reliable as the underlying data. — [Datalere: Driving results with conversational BI](https://datalere.com/articles/driving-results-with-conversational-bi-best-practices-for-power-and-casual-users) **[S — practitioner opinion]**

### Inferences
- Useful vs gimmicky, by verifiability: (a) NL → filtered view of an existing page, with the resulting filters shown as ordinary editable controls — the user verifies by reading the filter chips; (b) retrieval over manuals / past issues with the source passage shown — verifiable by reading the citation; (c) anomaly narration generated from *computed* statistics (the numbers come from code, the model only words them) — moderately safe; (d) free-form "explain this chart" from a rendered image or open-ended text-to-SQL over a large schema — weakest, per ChartHal and Spider 2.0.
- The Stepwise/Phasewise result supports showing the assistant's assumptions (which tool group, which period, which metric definition) as editable items before or beside the answer.
- Benchmarks move quickly; the figures above are a snapshot and should be re-checked before being quoted in 2027.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed field study of LLM assistants deployed inside semiconductor or manufacturing analytics tools was found.
- Spider 2.0 and Datalere figures come via secondary pages; the primary Spider 2.0 paper was not opened.
- No evidence found measuring whether "explain this chart" or anomaly narration reduces time-to-insight for experts.

## 8. Information density and visual design for experts on large desktop monitors

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is on colour: rainbow and red–green maps distort data and exclude colour-vision-deficient readers (about 8% of men), and perceptually uniform maps are the documented replacement — directly relevant to parametric wafer maps and AFM height images. For density and tables the guidance is expert opinion (NN/g); for loading states the response-time limits are well established but the evidence for skeleton screens is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **[E/O, peer-reviewed perspective]** Crameri, Shephard & Heron (Nature Communications, 2020): colour maps that distort data through uneven gradients or are unreadable with colour-vision deficiency "remain prevalent in science", notably rainbow-like and red–green maps; the remedy is perceptually uniform, CVD-accessible "scientific colour maps". — [The misuse of colour in science communication, PMC7595127](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7595127) **[S]**
- **[O]** Crameri (interview): most of the strong borders in a rainbow colour map "are artificial artifacts. They are not really there." — [University of Oslo, Titan: Improper use of colours can distort scientific data](https://titan.uio.no/english/2021/improper-use-colours-can-distort-scientific-data) **[S]**
- **[O]** Viridis was introduced (SciPy 2015) to replace jet because with jet small data changes sometimes produce large perceptual differences and vice versa, its lightness is non-monotonic, and it is not robust to colour-blind viewing. A 2022 NCEAS guide recommends viridis-family maps for continuous data and cites about 8% of men with colour-vision impairment. — [NCEAS: Colorblind Safe Color Schemes (PDF)](https://nceas.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Colorblind%20Safe%20Color%20Schemes.pdf) **[S]**
- **[E]** Colour discrimination limits for line series: Javed et al. note it is "plain difficult to differentiate eight (or sixteen) unique colors, particularly when each color is represented by a thin line". — [Javed, McDonnel & Elmqvist 2010](https://cs.au.dk/~elm/pdf/multilinevis.pdf)
- **[O]** NN/g guideline 8: make important information salient by removing non-essential elements (e.g. decorative icons beside numbers), not only by adding emphasis. — [NN/g complex applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/complex-application-design/)
- **[O]** Tables are more space-efficient than card layouts and better for comparing records and detecting patterns; freeze headers and the identifier column; light borders, zebra striping and hover highlight help users keep their place; first column should be a human-readable identifier; order columns by importance and keep related columns adjacent. — [Data Tables: Four Major User Tasks, NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/)
- **[O, long-established]** Response-time limits: 0.1 s feels instantaneous; 1 s keeps the user's flow of thought uninterrupted though the delay is noticed; 10 s is the limit for keeping attention on the task; beyond that show a percent-done indicator and expect users to switch tasks. — [NN/g: Response time topic page](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/response-time/); [NN/g video: The 3 Response Time Limits in Interaction Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/3-response-time-limits-interaction-design/) **[S]**
- **[E, mixed]** Skeleton screens: a 2017 Viget study (n=136) found the skeleton produced the *longest* perceived wait of three loaders tested; Mejtoft et al. (2018) found skeletons scored higher on perceived speed. — reported via [NN/g response-time topic search summary / secondary blogs](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/response-time/) **[S — neither primary study opened; treat as unresolved]**

### Inferences
- Parametric wafer maps and AFM/SEM-derived value maps should default to a perceptually uniform sequential map (viridis family or Crameri's maps) and a uniform diverging map when centred on a target or spec; rainbow should be at most an opt-in. Bin/status maps need a categorical palette checked for CVD plus a redundant cue (label, shape or pattern), since roughly 1 in 12 male engineers cannot rely on red vs green.
- Consistent status semantics (one colour = one meaning across all pages) follows from NN/g guideline 8 and CVD constraints but is not directly evidenced here.
- With series colours unreliable beyond about eight, tool-group charts with dozens of tools need highlight-on-select (one emphasised series over grey context) or small multiples rather than a 30-colour legend.
- For slow data sources: anything expected to take over 10 s needs determinate progress and should not block the rest of the page; whether to use skeletons or spinners under 10 s is not settled by evidence.
- Empty and error states: no source retrieved; the only supported principle is the NN/g/Shneiderman-style need to tell the user which filters are active so an empty result is explicable.

### Gaps
- No empirical source on optimal row density, font size or typography for dense expert tables on 1920×1080 was found; NN/g's table article does not address row density.
- Okabe-Ito and Paul Tol palette specifics surfaced only on low-authority pages and are not cited as facts.
- No evidence found specific to colour scales for wafer maps or to CVD-safe status colour sets in fab software.
- No sourced guidance found for empty/error state design for slow internal data sources.

## 9. Prioritising UX work in a small internal tool (dozens to a few hundred expert users)

### Takeaway
Two cheap methods are well suited and have published support for small populations: a top-tasks vote (a long task list, each user picks five) reliably exposes a steeply skewed distribution with modest samples, and heuristic evaluation by 3–5 evaluators finds most problems an individual would miss. The "5 users find 85%" rule is contested and is weakest exactly for complex, multi-role applications.

### Cited Findings
- **[O, practitioner method]** McGovern's Top Tasks: build a long list of candidate tasks, have users vote for their top five (5 points to the most important, down to 1); the method "produces reliable results from relatively small numbers of users" and reveals a few tasks that matter far more than the rest. An intranet study used 58 tasks and 242 respondents. — [Gerry McGovern Says "Manage the Tasks", Center Centre / UIE](https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=207); [Intranets: what staff really want, CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/cms/web-content/intranets-what-staff-really-want-001596.php) **[S]**
- **[O]** McGovern: top tasks are often "really boring" and unowned ("The top task is nearly always 'Find People'. But nobody owns it.") — i.e. teams under-invest in the mundane high-frequency task. — [Center Centre / UIE](https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=207) **[S]**
- **[E, case]** Red Hat applied Top Tasks to OpenShift with technical users (administrators, developers): gather tasks → shortlist → vote → visualise; a few tasks were far more critical than the great majority. — [OpenShift 4.5 top tasks, Red Hat blog](https://cloud.redhat.com/blog/openshift-4.5-top-tasks) **[S]**
- **[E]** Heuristic evaluation (Nielsen): individual evaluators found only 20–51% of usability problems across four experiments, but aggregates of three to five evaluators do well; more than five adds cost with little gain. — [Heuristic evaluation, Wikipedia (summarising Nielsen & Molich)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic_evaluation) **[S — secondary]**
- **[E, contested]** The "five users find ~85% of problems" figure from Nielsen & Landauer's model has been challenged (one paper argues five users may yield far fewer), and practitioner guidance limits it to one journey, one audience, one version — not a multi-role product. — [Search summary of the 5-user literature, e.g. UX Design Institute](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/?p=2652) **[S — low-authority secondary sources; primary papers not opened]**
- **[O]** NN/g: for complex applications users prefer to learn by doing and satisfice with the first method that works, so observed inefficiency (not stated dissatisfaction) is the signal to look for. — [NN/g complex applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/complex-application-design/)
- **[E, interview study]** Dashboard users' needs are more diverse than designers assume, which argues for asking them rather than inferring from page design. — [Tory et al. 2021](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/finding-their-data-voice-practices-and-challenges-dashboard-users) **[S]**

### Inferences
- A workable sequence for a small team: (1) usage logs to rank pages and find dead ones; (2) a top-tasks vote among the engineers to weight *importance* (logs show frequency, not importance, and under-count tasks the tool does badly); (3) task-based observation of a few engineers per role on the top three to five tasks, per role rather than five in total; (4) heuristic review of the top-task pages by three to five people.
- McGovern's "boring and unowned" observation suggests the highest-value improvement may be on the most-used mundane page (finding a tool or a measurement) rather than on the newest analytic view.
- With an expert population of a few hundred, a top-tasks vote can approach a census, which removes most sampling concerns.

### Gaps
- No source specific to prioritising UX in internal engineering/manufacturing tools was found; the support is from intranets and a developer platform.
- Primary Nielsen/Landauer and Nielsen/Molich papers were not opened; numbers above are via secondary pages.
- No evidence retrieved on usage-analytics methods (e.g. task-success instrumentation) for small expert populations.
